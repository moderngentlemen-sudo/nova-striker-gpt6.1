# Nova Striker World Codex Foundations

This is a compact continuity ledger for the separate version of Nova Striker. It preserves the supplied creative baseline while leaving new inventions visibly open. It is the foundation for a future World Codex, not a completed setting document.

The [original brief](source/development-brief.txt) remains available as a source snapshot. Subsequent user direction makes that brief a flexible guideline: meaningful departures may improve graphics, gameplay, or the overall experience, but must be checked with the user before adoption. The [decision register](DECISIONS.md) records that amendment. No original rule is silently removed by this ledger.

## Retained identity

Nova Striker is an original science-fiction action platformer with a side-scrolling foundation. Expressive movement, satisfying combat, differentiated characters, and one-to-four-player cooperation are central. A 2.5D presentation with 3D characters and environments is a candidate, with movement remaining on a clearly legible gameplay plane.

The visual direction is colorful, streamlined, premium science fiction with bold shapes and readable color language. Mega Man X, Overwatch, and Invincible provide inspiration rather than material to reproduce. A functioning and aspirational civilization can support danger, loss, and serious emotional stakes.

Unity and Blender are the working pipeline. Final platform availability, performance, and asset readiness require their own evidence.

## Nova and Echo

Nova and Echo are male close friends accepted into the Academy as Striker candidates. They share ambitions to serve, protect others, achieve greatness, and preserve their friendship. Affection, rivalry, different instincts, and dependence on each other shape their relationship.

Nova has lost someone important. That loss drives protective, cautious, preventative behavior and a preference for stopping threats at range. His potential flaw is overprotection and difficulty accepting necessary risks or another person's autonomy. The lost person's identity and the circumstances remain unknown.

Echo favors close combat and willingly places himself in danger. He believes hardship and enduring hits can build discipline. This characterization does not require avoidable damage to be optimal gameplay. His courage needs emotional substance and room for judgment.

| Character | Retained visual baseline | Retained gameplay direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nova | Male athletic Sentinel; blue and white armor with warm gold/orange energy; integrated forearm weapon; unhelmeted and full tactical helmet modes; articulated mobile silhouette. | Ranged pressure, charged attacks, interception, controlled positioning, active protection, Bulwark Pulse, helmet-enabled actions, and a heightened combat concept. |
| Echo | Pursuit Protocol Variation 02 field-ready direction; masculine athletic white/black armor with deep yellow-orange emissives; intact nano-adaptive scarf and protective collar; utility belt; bare face, ear-covering mask, and full helmet with semi-transparent amber visor. | Pursuit, close pressure, ground/air melee, counters, deliberate risk, dual gauntlet multi-tools combining into a staff with rifle utility and practical back storage. |

Nova is not silently returned to a hunter design. Echo's scarf is not a torn cape, and discarded wings or cape directions are not restored. The two characters share a coherent visual family while retaining different silhouettes and motion personalities. Exact approved image geometry is not available in this repository.

## Academy teams and suits

The principal story ensemble contains eight characters in two four-person operational units: Strike Team 1 and Strike Team 2. Each contains one Tank, one Striker 0, one Striker 1, and one Support. Striker 1 leads; Striker 0 is second-in-command. The numbered teams do not describe those ranks, and leadership does not automatically improve gameplay statistics.

Nova and Echo occupy the Striker track. Their permanent team placements and future Striker 0/Striker 1 assignments are unresolved. The remaining six principal characters, their personalities, suit powers, and assignments need development. Eight principal characters do not establish eight fully playable launch kits.

Graduating candidates who earn team placement receive the opportunity to travel to Ember Works and build a personal Strike Suit. A suit combines combat armor and an exoskeleton, provides protection and performance enhancement, and grants a unique signature ability. Team role, fighting style, suit ability, and Guardian-earned powers remain separate layers. One signature ability does not limit a character to one combat action.

## Regions and Guardians

The full-game planning baseline is six regions with three acts each. The first prototype is smaller.

| Region | Guardian | Retained identity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skyport | Aegis | Defense, protection, and counterplay |
| Ember Works | Cinder | Heat, industry, and rushdown pressure |
| Verdant Vault | Mycel | Biological growth and battlefield control |
| Cryo Relay | Rime | Crystalline forms, cold, and control |
| Storm Spire | Tempest | Aerial movement and electrical forces |
| Eclipse Core | Null | Spatial distortion, gravity, and clones |

The regions belong to an advanced civilization with energy systems, biomechanical technology, transit, artificial environments, and memory-related technology. Reality-bending phenomena near the Eclipse Core require limits and consequences. Guardians grant progression opportunities, but their allegiance, motives, and relationship to the crisis are unresolved.

## Retained play vocabulary

Movement includes running/backpedaling, crouching, powersliding, jump/double jump, wall cling/slide/jump, multidirectional dash, and Velocity Break. Velocity Break's exact behavior still needs a design decision.

Combat includes 360-degree aim, standard/charged ranged attacks, grounded/aerial melee, parry/perfect parry, character suit actions, Guardian abilities, and heightened states. Their costs, timing, priorities, and cancellations are design work.

The main campaign must be completable solo. One-to-four-player shared-screen play, downed/revive interactions, and Sync/Synergy remain design foundations. Online cooperation is a separate scope choice. Camera behavior, session interruptions, readability, and difficulty composition need early testing.

The broad platform ambition includes Windows, macOS, Linux, Steam Deck, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, Xbox One, Xbox Series S/X, PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Android, iOS, and iPadOS. This list does not establish simultaneous launch, development access, or proven performance. Cosmetic, microtransaction, and DLC capability likewise does not authorize real payment integration or determine a commercial model.

## New proposals awaiting adoption

The following ideas came from the fresh-start proposal and remain candidates:

- A preservation program begins correcting civilization toward recorded safe states, creating conflict with individual agency.
- Nova learns to make informed risks with others; Echo learns that accepting help and planning can express courage.
- An Academy prologue reaches graduation and suit creation before the main 18 acts.
- A provisional four-character playable roster begins with Nova, Echo, a rescue-oriented Tank, and an active field-technician Support.
- Guardian abilities offer shared new interactions while personal suits retain their signature combat identity.
- The first playable milestone is a blockout Control and Cooperation Lab with partial kits and two role shells.

These proposals do not settle a faction name, antagonist, resurrection rule, permanent assignment, final ability, costume alteration, or campaign chronology. See the prototype charter for candidate mechanics and the decision register for consequential choices.
